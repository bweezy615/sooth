Where the books disagree with each other.

An arb (arbitrage) is two books whose best prices on opposite sides add to less than 100% implied probability — betting both sides locks in a guaranteed profit regardless of outcome. A middle is two books whose lines leave a gap: land inside it and both bets win. Both are observations about disagreement, not predictions.

Arithmetic note: an arb's margin equals the gap between the summed implied probabilities and 100%, minus any limit or void risk — books can cancel obvious errors. A middle risks one side's vig (the book's margin) to chase a double win. Neither needs a forecast to be worth knowing about, which is why they are on this site.
